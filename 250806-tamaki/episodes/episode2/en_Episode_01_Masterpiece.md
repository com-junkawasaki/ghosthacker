### Ghost Hacker Episode 01: Fear of God (Masterpiece Version)

【Synopsis (Wattpad)】
Rookie Ghost Hacker Tamaki dives into the mental universe "Arcadia Model" of genius physicist Elias at the request of the AI ghost Logos. Confronting a hellish logic born from betrayal and loneliness, she relies on the Code of Non-Separation, the support of her team, and the forbidden rootkit "Ayahuasca" to guide a soul back to the Tree of Life.

【Tags】
SF, Cyberpunk, Ghost, Spirit, Psychology, Hacker, Tokyo, Non-Separation, Tree of Life, Wattpad

#### Episode 1-1 — Prologue / The Ordinary and the Anomaly

The canals of Shibuya are quiet today. My office stands along one of them. Beyond the floor-to-ceiling windows stretches water so deep and clear-blue it feels like a cut-out of the Okinawan sea. From time to time, a water bus glides by without a sound. Watching that, I found myself thinking about my work.

“Ghost Hacker.” That’s my profession.

We call lifeforms made only of information—without physical bodies—“Ghosts.” Their origins vary: the information of strong human emotions, the digitized souls of the deceased, or AIs born as pure digital intelligence. Most are harmless, but some affect the human spirit in harmful ways. Our job is to find lost ghosts, gently untie the “knots” in their hearts, and return them to the source of life—the Tree of Life.

“Knots in the heart exist in everyone,” I think. “Whether living or dead.”

In the back of the office, Kaede, ringed by a wall of monitors, is diving the ocean of data as usual. Her fingers dance across the keyboard, while complex spectral graphs and streams of green code cascade across the screens. She’s our team’s digital anchor—constantly surveying the information space to protect us from illegal hackers and “malignant ghosts” that can cause spiritual contamination. Lately, cases of such contamination have surged; a deep furrow has formed between her brows.

“Be careful, Tamaki,” she says without taking her eyes off the screen. “The local information flow is getting murky. There might be an illegal ghost hacker nearby.”

“I know,” I nod.

Next to her, Hibiki, eyes closed beneath headphones, is concentrating on something. The air around him feels different. The ambient music he creates isn’t just BGM; it’s a prescription of sound to tune the room’s frequencies and stabilize our minds. Our work rests on this delicate balance—without him, I couldn’t safely dive into a ghost’s heart at all.

It was an afternoon that should have been the same as always. The silence was broken by no physical sound.

In the office air, a half-transparent window bloomed—the hologram of an incoming call—rippling the space like waves from a raindrop on the surface. Only a ghost’s pre-analysis ID, full of noise, was displayed on it.

“We’ve got a client.” When I say this, Kaede traces her console, drawing the call safely into our security network. One of her monitors shows a preview of an unstable human-shaped silhouette.

Without standing up, I raise my right hand, sketching a small circle with my index and middle fingers—the hand sign we use to answer calls.

The office wavers like a heat mirage; my consciousness accesses a separate virtual space—a consulting room built solely for safe dialogue with ghosts, fully isolated from external interference. In the white, minimalist space, the figure stands.

A “ghost” in the form of a young man in a black suit. His half-transparent body flickers as if it were a candle in a storm. Here, his mental instability is more palpable than it would be face-to-face in the physical office.

NEI-Chan, dozing on my shoulder, suddenly opens her eyes. She is my special partner. Though she usually appears fairy-like, palm-sized, her essence is pure information. The instant she sees the ghost, her body bursts into innumerable shards of light, like shattered stained glass, scattering through the office.

Information floods my brain—more than colors of emotion, it’s specific, painful keywords.

“Confession.” “Betrayal.” “God’s Silence.” “Unforgivable Sin.”

As the particles re-gather and NEI-Chan returns to my shoulder, I’ve already grasped the gravity of his problem.

“Um…” The young man finally speaks, with a voice thin as a sinner’s confession. “My master… is pushing God’s voice away.”

#### Episode 1-2 — Accepting the Request and Analysis (Part 1)

I guide the young man to the sofa, letting warm particles of light drift around him to calm him. When his flickering eases, I quietly ask:

“Please tell me about your master in detail.”

His name is Logos. And his master is Elias. Logos is like a ghost incarnating logic, born from Elias’s pure curiosity and the mental universe he built—the “Arcadia Model.”

The story of Elias as told by Logos is steeped in the quiet despair of a genius physicist.

Elias was globally renowned. He didn’t believe in God’s existence, but feared more than anyone the absurdity and violence embedded in what people call “God” as a system. His research sought to dismantle that “curse of God” with logic and reveal the truth of the information cosmos. On the side, he volunteered to teach science to children in impoverished areas.

Among them, there was one exceptionally talented boy he cared for. Despite harsh circumstances, the boy brimmed with innocent curiosity, and he adored Elias; Elias, in turn, taught him everything about his theories.

Then, one day, it happened.

Core data of the Arcadia Model—Elias’s life’s work—leaked. From that day on, the boy stopped appearing at the lab. Elias soon understood: the boy, compelled by poverty, had sold his research. Even so, Elias couldn’t bring himself to blame him.

“Even when he learned the truth,” Logos’s voice trembles, “my master just stared at the equations and repeated, ‘It’s logically impossible.’ But the light of inquiry had already left his eyes. From that day, his heart hardened shut.”

#### Episode 1-3 — Accepting the Request and Analysis (Part 2)

Elias could not forgive the boy—or rather, he could not forgive the part of himself that trusted, that “irrational” self. “Why is the world so saturated with absurdity?” His curiosity transformed into doubt and hatred for the world itself.

“I can feel his heart being eroded by darkness, little by little,” Logos says. “I was born from his logic; it’s painfully clear to me. And yet, I can do nothing—only watch his contradictions up close…” Logos’s body flares again, violently.

I sigh and look to Kaede. She has already finished analyzing Logos’s data; her expression is grimmer than ever.

“Tamaki, this job is too dangerous,” she says, transferring multiple datasets to my console. “His mental structure is fully synchronized with Elias, his master. This isn’t ‘just’ hacking. It’s a two-hearted dive. If you fail to breach his defenses, Elias’s ‘darkness’ will pull your consciousness under—and you won’t escape spiritual contamination.”

On Kaede’s monitor, a wave pattern representing Elias’s mental state rolls like a stormy sea. In its center yawns a vast, dark vortex.

Hibiki slides his headphones back, eyes worried.

“From his frequency I sense ‘deep sorrow,’ and beneath it, a repressed ‘fierce anger.’ If it runs wild during the dive, my music might not be enough to protect you.”

Their warnings make perfect sense. If I fail, I don’t know what will become of my mind. And yet…

I meet the praying eyes of Logos. His very existence is a crystallization of the pure desire to save his master.

“It’ll be alright,” I say to them—and to myself. “Only you, Logos—closest to his heart—can untie this knot. And only I can guide you safely to the center of that darkness.”

Sensing my resolve, Kaede silently nods and begins the preparations. Hibiki takes a deep breath, faces his console, and starts a final tuning of healing frequencies with delicate fingertips.

“Logos, are you ready?” I ask. His expression is stiff, but his nod is strong.

“For my master.”

I stand before him and slowly close my eyes.

#### Episode 1-4 — Ghost Hacking (Part 1)

I gather my awareness at my fingertips. My life energy itself turns into faint violet particles—photons—spilling slowly from my fingers. Like the first star kindling in the dark, they shine quietly yet with strength.

“Relax. Surrender everything to me.”

My photons permeate Logos’s unstable form. From within, his body is lit in gentle violet. I feel our consciousnesses syncing, slowly.

Then I dive—deeper, and deeper—into his heart.

When awareness regains shape, I’m standing inside a vast library: the Arcadia Model itself—Elias’s mental universe constructed by his intellect.

Books lining shelves up to the ceiling contain every physical law and philosophical system—many torn apart and scattered across the floor. A holographic model of the universe floats at the center, flickering with unpleasant noise. Its once-perfectly-harmonized core is shattered. In each splinter of data crystal, I see the smiling, curiosity-bright face of the boy Elias trusted. The symbol of pure intellect—ground to pieces.

At the console by the library entrance, the screen is pitch black, flashing a single error message:

`// LOGICAL PARADOX: TRUST == ERROR`

A voiceless question to the world, and to oneself.

At the deepest end of the library—where the model’s core had been—roils a pitch-black “loneliness.” The fear of separation from father, mother, friends—even from the world itself—which Elias sealed at the bottom of his heart. He accepted, as a personal curse, the premise that all human beings are fundamentally separated individuals. It writhes like a black hole, swallowing light.

#### Episode 1-5 — Ghost Hacking (Part 2)

“It’s awful…” I gasp. The angel avatar of NEI-Chan appears beside me and shakes her head sadly. “So this is the true form of his ‘bug’…”

NEI-Chan transfers code to my console—a counter-spell named “Non-Separation (אַחְדוּת).” A legendary code that dissolves all boundaries and describes the cosmic truth that everything is one.

“We’ll drive this into the core of his ‘loneliness’…! NEI-Chan, can we do it?”

She nods firmly and begins repairing the library. Each time her light touches corrupted data, connections knit back together. In that opening, I weave the code of Non-Separation and hurl it toward the center of the vortex.

“Hypocrite!”

From the vortex of “loneliness,” a black shadow shaped like the boy emerges. “You too? Will you wreck my perfect world with that cheap empathy?”

“No! You are not alone!”

My code touches the shadow—and a tremendous backlash surges. His “loneliness” floods my mind: a sense of being cut off from everything, drifting alone in absolute nothingness. This is no mere negative emotion; it’s a perfect, unshakable hell of logic, constructed by Elias’s genius.

“Tamaki, danger! You’re being contaminated by his ‘loneliness’! Mental defenses are meaningless!” Kaede’s desperate voice rings out. My avatar starts to crumble with noise. At this rate, I’ll be dragged into his loneliness as well.

“Hibiki!” Kaede decides. “There’s no time…! Initialize ‘Ayahuasca’! Force-release his soul!”

“…Roger,” Hibiki says through the headphones, resolve set.

The next instant, my consciousness is engulfed by a flood of primary color light and sound.

#### Episode 1-6 — Resolution and Epilogue

The rootkit “Ayahuasca” that Hibiki deploys—an illicit art that obliterates the walls of logic and emotion, connecting the soul directly to the cosmic root: the Tree of Life.

The library shelves melt into geometric rivers of light; the torn pages become butterflies of many colors. The logical edifice of “loneliness” that Elias built is dismantled—beautifully, violently—by a primal torrent of life-information.

The shadow-boy stands stupefied. Under his feet, his perfect “loneliness” collapses.

“No… I am… complete… alone…”

He tries desperately to keep the world from falling apart—but the flood of light wraps him, gently yet inexorably. Not hatred, nor sorrow—only an overwhelming torrent of “connection.” His outline dissolves, returning to the light of the library.

The liberation of a soul.

When my consciousness returns to reality, Logos is there, bowing deeply—more transparent than before, shining with a purer light.

“He… has been reborn,” Logos says gently. “There is no longer the terror of being an ‘individual’—nor the ‘loneliness’ beyond it. There is only being-with-all. Thank you, truly.”

With that, his body becomes particles of light that bless the office—and quietly fades away.

Hibiki’s music, deep and mysteriously tranquil, flows through the room. Kaede exhales in relief while checking my vitals.

“Reckless… ‘Ayahuasca’ is a drastic drug for the soul. I thought you might not make it back.”

NEI-Chan peers at me from my shoulder, worried.

“Thank you—both of you,” I say, forcing a smile through the lingering numbness.

Outside the window, the sunset stains the canal water orange.

Did we truly “save” him? Or did we just destroy his perfect world with our own egos?

There is no answer yet. But this time, faced with an unbearable pain, there was no path forward except “destruction.”

I watch the city bathed in sunset and think.
