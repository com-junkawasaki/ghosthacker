# S1E2-1 — The Fear of Not Being Able to Die

- POV: Me (Tamaki / Mizuno Tamaki)
- Beat: New Request

This client feared not being able to die.

“Will I continue forever? Like this, again and again…?”

His voice rasped like dry leaves. The prison of eternal recurrence—its terror gnawed at his Ghost.

Nei-chan, beside me, spoke quietly: “He believes too strongly that he is an individual. So endless samsara feels like punishment.”

“Right,” I agree. “He’s forgotten that he’s part of the cosmos itself.”

Our work is to help him soften the border between himself and the world and return to the greater flow. Wu wei, ziran. Simply, to be as it is.

“That’s also why you always protect ‘feeling good,’ Tamaki,” Nei-chan says.

“Mm. Because our ‘feeling good’ links to the ‘feeling good’ of the next universe.”

Once you know the self-boundary is an illusion, life and death become waves. I face the client, quietly, deeply, and match breath.


# S1E2-2 — The Endless Corridor

- POV: Me (Tamaki / Mizuno Tamaki)
- Beat: Dive / Confrontation

We dive into a corridor that goes on forever. The same doors, the same windows, the same light. No matter how far we go, nothing changes, no end. His mind is trapped in this loop.

“He’s separating self and world completely,” Nei-chan analyzes. “That’s why the idea of the self continuing eternally becomes a prison.”

At the heart of his fear is the dead end of dualism. I stop mid-hall and touch the wall. Cold—but not an illusion. It’s real because he believes it hard enough to be reality.

“We’ll have to melt this wall.”

I raise ASC control one notch. The particles of the scenery coarsen; the seams of the world show. I search for the frayed edge of the loop, to open an exit outside what he thinks is “himself.”


# S1E2-3 — For the Next Universe

- POV: Me (Tamaki / Mizuno Tamaki)
- Beat: Resolution / Afterthoughts

“Step outside the wall.”

My voice ripples through his mental world. The walls of the endless corridor crumble like sand. Beyond them: a cosmos where stars are born.

“You are both a distinct being and this universe itself.”

I speak to him. The line he thought was “self” never existed—he only believed it so.

“It isn’t that you ‘cannot die.’ You will simply continue, changing form, as the universe does.”

The outline of his Ghost dissolves into starlight. Fear drains; relief rises. He is released from the prison of infinity and returns to the greater flow.

We end the session and return to the office. Outside the window, the same night view of the city of water.

Nei-chan: “Our work is… grand, isn’t it?”

“Yes. So the next universe can be a little more ‘feeling good.’ We’re only helping a bit.”

I add a new line to my notebook: ‘Boundaries are an illusion.’ It tells the whole story of today’s session.
