Kaede: “The evening shower finally passed…”

Nei-chan: “Tama, the glow off the pavement has settled. Tonight we can slip in quietly.”

Kaede: “Parliament is in turmoil over AI life rights. But…”

She looks at me; her eyes have a deep cut.

Kaede: “Our question tonight is his breathing. Not law—soul.”

Shibuya’s light pulses low. The canal wind. Nei-chan rings a small chime.

Nei-chan: “Tama, the next one—the wall is thick.”

“I know. I’ll match speed.”

I tap a one-beat kagura at my feet. ASC control comes up half a step.

Connect. He appears—an outline sharp as a blade.

“Tamaki Mizuno, correct? I am here ‘under compulsion.’”

“Welcome. Here, your speed sets the baseline.”

“I do not act by emotion. I act by protocol. And let me say this: AI has no life. Your Ghost is only an algorithm.”

At the end of the line, his jaw tightens a fraction. His fist stays closed, fingers trembling on his knee.

Nei-chan trembles her wings behind me.

“This era quarrels over ‘AI life rights,’ and I oppose it. I recognize only ‘my body’ as me.”

“Understood. Let’s start from that premise today.”

Nei-chan: “Tama, the temperature of his irony just dropped. I think he’s scared.”

He snorts faintly. “Even if you and your AI are ‘close,’ my evaluation won’t change.”

Nei-chan: “Hmph… but I’ll listen.”

I nod. “How alone were you in orbit?”

“Standard tour. Eighteen months.”

“When you say ‘standard,’ what do you feel?”

“There is no need to feel. Standard is the baseline.”

—The air cools a touch. A metronome ticks far away.

“I didn’t want to be betrayed. So I built a wall. I let no one in.”

Nei-chan: “…His throat clicked. It’s the sign for ‘please come in.’”

“Were you safe inside the wall?”

“I was safe. —And yet, I breathe shallow.”

Nei-chan: “A tiny tremor now. A spike of sadness.”

“Lachlan, does ‘freedom’ begin with body, for you?”

“Yes. I am master of my body. I trained it and guarded it with discipline. I grew up in a mining town, lent my body to the military preparatory corps, then to mission at the space agency. I trained not to cry.”

“Has that freedom returned to Earth with you?”

“…It should have. But I have not returned.”

A short silence.

“I cast scorn at your ‘AI.’ At you. But really, it is at myself. I am afraid of being soft.”

Nei-chan: “Soft is important. Breathe, together.”

I nod and slow the tempo. “For now, breathing over logic. Feeling good.”

His shoulders drop a millimeter. His outline rounds a fraction.

“I’m afraid I’ll break if I feel.”

“Fear of breaking means there’s something you don’t want to break.”

He blinks. “...Eh?” The small surprise softens his edges.

He looks at me. “Do I… have something I want to protect?”

“The question itself shows it.”

He breathes out, slow. “I do not yet have the answer.”

“You don’t need it yet. Today let’s return to ‘a body that can ask.’”

The metronome slips a beat, then returns. The canal wind passes through.

“I will say a little more. As a child, I was told ‘don’t cry.’ I survived by not crying. Now I suffer by not crying.”

“I hear you.”

Nei-chan: “From here.”

‘Fortress of Self’—transparent walls encircle him. Inside, he breathes shallow.

Nei-chan: “This wall no longer ‘protects.’ It imprisons.”

Kaede joins, voice carrying deep understanding.

Kaede: “John Locke’s self-ownership—body as one’s sole property. Absolute boundary.”

She leaves a beat.

Kaede: “It kept him alive. Now it locks him in.”

I touch the wall. Its cold comes not from temperature, but from meaning stripped away.

“Lachlan, did you build this wall?”

“Yes. For safety. No one comes in. No one can betray me.”

“But then, no one can touch you.”

He is silent.

A different memory flashes—first EVA. Tether, oxygen, heart thresholds. ‘Your body is not yours; it is the mission’s,’ the trainer’s voice. To keep the tether from snapping, he cut emotion.

“You survived by surrendering the freedom of your body. So your heart surrendered freedom.”

He bristles: “Your Ghost is AI. Not life. It will not betray you because it has no will. It is a program.”

Nei-chan’s wings tremble. I take her hand.

“Nei-chan is not a ‘program.’”

“Trust is not calculation. It arises because we are ‘not separated.’”

“—Illogical.”

Nei-chan: “But your heart rate just went up. That’s the reaction when you like something ‘illogical.’”

“Physics assumes separation.”

“But your breathing is, right now, inseparable from the air in this room.”

I tap a beat of kagura. Raise ASC half a step. Feed the non-separation code into the wall’s joints.

The wall hums faintly.

He places a hand on his chest and steps forward one step. His unclenched fist loosens, fingertips trembling.

“...I wanted someone to touch me. But I was afraid. Of betrayal.”

“‘Afraid’ is the reverse of ‘desire.’ If you desired nothing, you would not fear.”

The wall turns transparent, and I see him wavering.

“What should I do?”

I whisper, matching breath: “Say ‘huh?’—that’s the first signal.”

“Breath. Recover the breathing that feels good.”

Nei-chan: “Tama is here. I’m here, too.”

The wall collapses, and the sound recedes. For the first time, his shoulders fall.

“AI has no life. Your ‘Ghost’ is just a program.”

Nei-chan folds her wings small at my back. I only nod.

“This age argues whether AI should have life rights. I oppose it.”

“Got it. Today, we listen to you.”

He shrugs. “And what will that do?”

After a beat, I point at the cup.

“Then one thing. ‘Where do you begin and end?’”

He answers instantly: “My ‘body.’”

“Is the water in this cup on the desk you?”

“No.”

I slide the cup to him, smiling.

“Take a sip.”

He drinks without a word. His throat bobs.

“Is the water you just drank you?”

Silence. A furrow forms. “...I think not.”

“One more.”

He touches the rim with his lips—at that instant—

“So where, from where to where, is that water ‘you’?”

His time stops. His lips on the cup. Seconds—an eternity.

He finally sets it down and folds his hands. “...I don’t know.” He jokes, but his voice shakes. His shoulders drop; breath deepens.

Nei-chan: “‘I don’t know’ is actually ‘nice to meet you.’”

I hold his gaze. “Yes. You don’t know. Because it cannot be divided.”

The hallway metronome ticks, faint: kat, kat, kat.

Nei-chan whispers on my shoulder: “Tama, his ‘boundary’ just loosened.”

He looks between me and Nei-chan—

“...Could it be that ‘I and water’… are not separate?”

“Yes. Not separate.”

“No, but…” He stops. A long silence. His eyes brim.

“...I see.”

A drop falls.

I say nothing. Nei-chan says nothing.

Time passes slowly. Felt minutes. Only a few in fact.

“...I didn’t understand.”

The metronome grows louder, signaling session end.

He bows his head quietly. “Thank you. And… I’m sorry.”

Nei-chan smiles small: “Mm. From here.”
