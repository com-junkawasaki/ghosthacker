Kaede: “Morning river fog…”

Kaede: “Pressure is dropping. On days like this, emotions destabilize easily. Deep breaths, Tamaki.”

Nei-chan: “Tama, it’s a couple today. The air’s a little hot.”

Morning light touches the Shibuya canal. Nei-chan is making something in the kitchen—she doesn’t eat, but likes to cook for me.

Nei-chan: “Tama, today’s tough. Couple session.”

I pour coffee and open the ToL file.

Leo Kazan, 38, Director of a humanitarian NGO. Millia Stuart, 30, digital artist.

Kaede’s note is attached:

“They say they ‘love’ each other. But Millia repeats self-harm; Leo ‘tries to save’ her and is exhausted. High likelihood of co-dependence.”

Nei-chan sits beside me: “Tama, what is ‘love’?”

I smile. “Love. I know it—but right now, I’m ‘waiting.’”

Nei-chan: “Ehh? Then I don’t get it!”

Kaede: “You two, session starts now.”

Kaede joins, more tense than usual.

Kaede: “Tamaki… this one is hard. I’ve seen it many times in ER—couples who hurt each other under the name of ‘love.’”

She leaves a beat.

Kaede: “Leo’s Ghost is Adam Smith’s Theory of Moral Sentiments—seeking ‘sympathy’ from others. Millia’s Ghost is J.S. Mill’s liberty of the individual, plus Japan’s patriarchal ‘chain of ancestors.’ Two Ghosts are tearing her apart.”

Deep compassion in Kaede’s tone.

Kaede: “They’re trying to ‘save’ each other—and in doing so, they’re hurting each other. In triage terms, near collapse together.”

Hibiki’s frequency enters—warm, with a trace of sadness.

Hibiki: “Tamaki… listen to their bands.”

Quiet, earnest.

Hibiki: “They are completely async. They use the ‘same words,’ but the meanings don’t match. In music terms, it’s like playing two different songs at once.”

I inhale, give Nei-chan a cue, and tap a small kagura. ASC prepped.

The display blurs; the two appear. Leo is passionate; Millia is delicate; and yet… hollowed.

“You’re Tamaki Mizuno? I’m Leo Kazan. This is Millia.”

Nei-chan (small): “Passion’s strong. Millia’s guard is thin.”

Leo’s voice is warm, with fatigue behind it.

Millia smiles small. A fragile smile.

“I heard you two ‘love each other.’”

Leo answers at once: “Yes. I love Millia. She is… everything to me.”

His fingers squeeze her hand unconsciously. Millia pulls back for an instant, then returns.

Millia’s smile freezes a little.

Nei-chan whispers: “Tama, Millia’s emotional metrics… not ‘love’—‘guilt’ is strong.”

I ask Millia:

“Millia, do you… love Leo?”

Her gaze wavers. She says, small:

“...I’m afraid of being ‘loved’ by Leo.”

Leo’s throat clicks. “...I’m sorry.”

Leo: “Eh…?”

Leo’s face collapses.

“Millia… why? Weren’t we here to decide work choices today? We have to explain to the board, to family, friends—everyone—”

Tamaki: “Who made the booking?”

Millia: “...I did. Watching Leo ‘enjoy’ struggling makes me happy… and anxious.”

Tamaki: “Happy?”

Millia: “...I like Leo’s family stories. They’re warm.”

Leo (bright): “People call me sunny. But I think I can handle it positively.”

Leo: “My family always supports me, and I have many close friends. So—I thought it would be fine.”

I breathe in. The real work starts here.

“Leo, you think ‘sympathy’ is something you give, don’t you?”

He looks at me, startled.

“Millia, you think ‘love’ is a debt you must repay, don’t you?”

Tears pool in her eyes.

Nei-chan: “Tama, both of them think ‘love’ is a transaction.”

I turn to them.

“You’re mixing up ‘love’ and ‘credit.’ And you’re forgetting ‘trust.’”

Leo asks: “...What’s the difference?”

I smile.

“We’ll find it now.”

Millia (elbows him): “...”

Tamaki: “This is the economy of ‘friends’—the economy of sympathy and trust.”

Tamaki: “‘Because she’s cute.’ ‘Because it’s Tamaki.’ Relationship creates value. A value of emotion and sympathy that doesn’t arise with strangers.”

Millia (pinches)

Leo: “Ow-ow-ow!”

Kaede: “Continue same session.”

Leo leans in. Millia pulls back slightly.

“‘Credit’ is a transaction. You give me something, I give you something. Equivalent exchange.”

Nei-chan adds: “Banks lend with ‘credit.’ They hand over money on the premise it comes back.”

I continue.

“‘Trust’ is not calculation. It is ‘not being separate.’ I trust Nei-chan because she is a part of me.”

Millia speaks small: “...But we are… separate.”

I smile.

“Are you sure?”

I ask her:

“Millia, when you look at Leo, what do you feel?”

Her eyes fill.

“...When I see him suffering… I suffer.”

“Why?”

“...Because it’s my fault.”

I shake my head.

“No. Because you and Leo are ‘not separate.’ You feel his pain as your own.”

Leo blurts: “...Is that sympathy?”

I smile.

“No. It’s ‘the extended self.’”

Leo: “...Eh?”

Nei-chan: “We got an ‘eh?’”

Kaede joins with deep insight:

Kaede: “Adam Smith, in Theory of Moral Sentiments, frames sympathy as ‘standing in the other’s place.’”

She leaves a beat.

Kaede: “But that presupposes the boundary of ‘individuals.’ Leo, you try to understand Millia ‘as an individual.’ That, paradoxically, isolates her.”

The compassion of an ER doctor who’s seen this pain many times.

Hibiki’s frequency enters—warm, a touch sad.

Hibiki: “Leo… listen to your ‘sympathy.’”

Gentle, but truthful.

Hibiki: “It is actually a kind of ‘credit.’ ‘If I understand you, you’ll understand me’—that’s the sound of a transaction.”

Leo pales.

“...I was making Millia… an object of transaction?”

His voice scrapes. His gaze falls to his knee. Millia, unconsciously, reaches for the water on the table and wets her lips.

I speak quietly.

“You tried to ‘save’ her. But she didn’t want to be ‘saved.’ She just wanted a presence that is ‘not separate.’”

Nei-chan approaches Millia.

“Millia, you cut your wrists to sever the ‘chain of ancestors.’ But a chain like that cannot be cut alone.”

Millia’s voice trembles: “...I want to be alone. But being alone is scary…”

I take her hand.

“Millia, you’re imprisoned by the boundary called ‘individual.’ But ‘individual’ is an illusion. You and Leo are already ‘not separate.’”

I look to Leo.

“Leo, you are ‘not separate’ from Millia too. That is why her pain exhausts you.”

I tell them:

“You think ‘love’ is ‘the feeling of an individual.’ Love is ‘the extended self.’ You are already one. Acknowledge that.”

Leo’s eyes fill.

“...I tried to respect Millia ‘as an individual.’ But that isolated her.”

Millia sobs: “...I wanted to be free. But ‘freedom’… was loneliness.”

Leo leans toward her. She rests just a little on his shoulder.

Nei-chan smiles: “Freedom does not contradict ‘not being separate.’ Tama is part of me, and I’m part of Tama. And we are still different.”

I ask them:

“As an ‘extended self,’ how do you want to live?”

Leo smiles—first real one: “...I want to be with Millia ‘not separate.’ No transactions. Just… together.”

Millia smiles—first true smile: “...I want to be ‘not separate’ with Leo. And I want to cherish ‘me’ too.”

I smile.

“That’s right. That is ‘love.’”

Tamaki: “Someone you only passed once. You will never meet again. —Would you sell them for 100 million?”

Leo: “Hm… in that case, I might.”

Millia (small): “...I would.”

Tamaki: “Right. Value differs by relation. Relationships have value.”

I leave a beat.

Tamaki: “Financial capital is a 2,000 trillion-dollar market. Social capital is worth thousands of times more. The world’s economy runs on sympathy and relation.”

Leo nods. Millia looks down.

Tamaki: “Next question. —Would you sell one of your eyes for 100 million?”

Leo’s face changes.

Leo: “...An eye!? 100 million….” He thinks. “...I’d struggle… but I wouldn’t.”

Millia (small): “...I might.”

Leo turns to her, shocked: “What? You’d sell?”

Tamaki: “Final question.”

Tamaki: “Would you sell your family for 100 million?”

Leo: “Eh—that I would never.”

A long silence.

Nei-chan: “Tama, Millia’s emotional metrics… shaking hard.”

Tamaki: “What about a billion?”

Leo: “No!” Firm. Millia—

Millia: “...I would.”

Leo: “Eh?” He looks at her.

Millia looks down, shoulders shivering, then bursts into tears.

Leo: “Wh-what’s wrong?” He’s lost.

Millia sobs without answer, then forces out words:

Millia: “For one yen… for any amount… I want to sell them…”

I do not touch that, and speak gently to her instead.

Tamaki: “Yes. The way to tell the economy of love and relationship is ‘can it be sold or not.’”

I meet her eyes.

Tamaki: “Whether you feel it as ‘yourself’ or not.”

Leo: “I—I see…” He still doesn’t know what to do here.

I turn to Millia.

Tamaki: “But even if it’s ‘family,’ it isn’t that you must not sell.”

She stops crying a little.

Millia: “...It’s okay to sell?”

Her voice shakes.

Tamaki: “Yes. Family too—whether they are ‘you’ or not. You decide.”

I continue softly.

Tamaki: “In Japanese culture, family is assumed unconditionally to be oneself. In the West, a partner is sworn as oneself before God.”

I hold her gaze.

Tamaki: “You don’t have to love someone who lies, saying ‘I love you.’”

Leo startles, lost.

Tamaki: “Someone who seizes your sympathy and soul, who binds you—sympathy extraction. That must not be allowed.”

Millia: “But… even if I want to sell, to cut, I can’t cut it off…” Despair.

Tamaki: “Yes. Because it has become part of your body.”

I speak quietly.

Tamaki: “That’s ‘complex.’ A psycho-somatic complex—become spirit. Souls have adhered.”

Tamaki: “Even if bodies are apart, souls remain bound. Cutting the soul is the same as cutting your body.”

I leave a small pause.

Tamaki: “Cut the soul and it bleeds. Cutting hurts.”

At that moment I see faint white lines on her wrist. Scars of self-harm.

I lower my eyes and say nothing.

Nei-chan (small): “Tama… I saw.”

Tamaki (small): “Mm. Not yet.”

I face Millia.

Tamaki: “You have courage. But for a proper cut, leave it to a specialist.”

Millia: “...What should I do, Dr. Tamaki?” A small voice with a hint of hope.

Tamaki: “Mm. Procedure… anesthesia and surgery. Let me ask.”

Light returns to her eyes.

I nod.

“Yes. There is a specialist. Dr. Itako.”

I meet her gaze.

“—You can be free.”

Leo slowly takes her hand.

Leo: “Millia. I’ll… be with you.” Gentle and firm.

Millia smiles through tears: “...Doctor, please.”

I nod.

“It’s okay. You’re not alone. Leo, and all of us, are with you.”

Nei-chan: “Tama, their hands—holding. Warm.”

Leo takes her hand again—this time, without hesitation.

Leo: “Millia. I’ll… be with you. Through any operation. After, too.”

Millia smiles through tears: “...Thank you.”

Nei-chan, gently: “Tama, their hands—holding. Warm.”

I look at them.

“Next step is Dr. Itako’s session. —For today, that’s all. Talk slowly, together.”

Leo nods. Millia nods quietly.

The room’s light slowly returns.

Millia’s face softens, just a little. A burden lifts, just a little.

Leo: “...Millia, I don’t know what happens next, but… we’ll move forward together.”

Millia: “...Yes. Thank you, Leo.”

For the first time, relief colors her voice.

Their outlines stabilize.

For the first time—they truly look at each other.

I smile, quietly.

“It’s okay. You’re no longer alone. A new road will open.”

Nei-chan: “Tama, so warm. The color of hope. Sparkly.”

Leo holds her hand; she squeezes back.

A quiet trust flows between them.

I nod.

“Yes. It’ll be okay. One step at a time.”

Millia smiles small. Tears not yet dry, but—hope in her eyes.
