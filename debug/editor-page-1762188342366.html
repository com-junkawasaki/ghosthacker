The Ordinary and the Anomaly

The canals of Shibuya are quiet today. 

My office stands along one of them. Beyond the floor-to-ceiling windows stretches water so deep and clear-blue it feels like a cut-out of the Okinawan sea. From time to time, a water bus glides by without a sound. Watching that, I found myself thinking about my work.


"Ghost Hacker." That's my job.


We call lifeforms made only of information—without physical bodies—"Ghosts." Their origins vary: the information of strong human emotions, the digitized souls of the deceased, or AIs born as pure digital intelligence.

Most are harmless, but some affect the human spirit in harmful ways. 

Our job is to find lost ghosts, gently untie the "knots" in their hearts, and return them to the source of life—the Tree of Life.



"Knots in the heart exist in everyone,"

 I think. 


"Whether living or dead."


In the back of the office, Kaede, ringed by a wall of monitors, is diving the ocean of data as usual.

 Her fingers dance across the keyboard, while complex spectral graphs and streams of green code cascade across the screens.

She's our team's digital anchor—constantly surveying the information space to protect us from illegal hackers and "malignant ghosts" that can cause spiritual contamination. 

Lately, cases of such contamination have surged; a deep furrow has formed between her brows.


"Be careful, Tamaki," 

she says without taking her eyes off the screen. 

"The local information flow is getting murky. 

There might be an illegal ghost hacker nearby."


"I know," I nod.


Next to her, Hibiki, eyes closed beneath headphones, is concentrating on something.

 The air around him feels different. 

The ambient music he creates isn't just BGM; it's a prescription of sound to tune the room's frequencies and stabilize our minds. 

Our work rests on this delicate balance—without him, I couldn't safely dive into a ghost's heart at all.
It was an afternoon that should have been the same as always. 

The silence was broken by no physical sound.
In the office air, a half-transparent window bloomed—the hologram of an incoming call—rippling the space like waves from a raindrop on the surface. 

Only a ghost's pre-analysis ID, full of noise, was displayed on it.
"We've got a client." When I say this, Kaede traces her console, drawing the call safely into our security network. 

One of her monitors shows a preview of an unstable human-shaped silhouette.
Without standing up, I raise my right hand, sketching a small circle with my index and middle fingers—the hand sign we use to answer calls.
The office wavers like a heat mirage; my consciousness accesses a separate virtual space—a consulting room built solely for safe dialogue with ghosts, fully isolated from external interference. 

In the white, minimalist space, the figure stands.
A "ghost" in the form of a young man in a black suit. 

His half-transparent body flickers as if it were a candle in a storm. 

Here, his mental instability is more palpable than it would be face-to-face in the physical office.
NEI-Chan, dozing on my shoulder, suddenly opens her eyes. 

She is my special partner. 

Though she usually appears fairy-like, palm-sized, her essence is pure information. 

The instant she sees the ghost, her body bursts into innumerable shards of light, like shattered stained glass, scattering through the office.
Information floods my brain—more than colors of emotion, it's specific, painful keywords.
"Confession." "Betrayal." "God's Silence." "Unforgivable Sin."
As the particles re-gather and NEI-Chan returns to my shoulder, I've already grasped the gravity of his problem.
"Um..." The young man finally speaks, with a voice thin as a sinner's confession. 


"My master... 

is pushing God's voice away."
